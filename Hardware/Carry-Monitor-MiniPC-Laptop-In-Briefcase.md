## DIY 18.5 Inch Desktop Monitor Stand

* HP V194 18.5 inch monitor's dimension
  * Width: 17.5 Inch
  * Height (Monitor Only): 11 Inch
  * Height (With Stand): 13.5 Inch

* For making DIY monitor stand, do not use screw, instead use hinge.

* Use SS (Stainless Steel), not MS (Mild Steel) or plain steel. Because SS steel is stronger and lighter.

* VESA Plate: Monitor VESA mount has two sizes, 75x75cm and 100x100cm. According to VESA size, make a SS plate and make 4 holes for screws. 

* Vertical Stand: Attach verical stand with "VESA Plate" using hinge, so that monitor can be tilted. Hinge must be strong so that, it can hold tilted monitor.

# Concept

## DIY Stainless-Steel Portable Monitor Stand


> ChatGPT Question: 
> 
> on a base plate, there is perpendicular 3 inch 1.5mm 1inch ss box pipe. 
> another 10 inch 1.2mm 1.25 inch ss box pipe is fit on top (inner/outer) of 
> it. at the farmost top another 3 inch 1.5mm 1inch ss box pipe attached with 
> VESA mount including hp v194 18.5 inch monitor. Will this wobble?

Yes. If you change the **base to 8" × 8" × 1.5 mm** and make the **lower and upper 1" × 1" tubes 1.5 mm**, while keeping the 10" outer tube at 1.2 mm, the stand becomes noticeably stiffer with only a small weight penalty.

 Assuming SS304/316 at roughly 8.0 g/cm³:

 ***### Weight comparison***

 | Component | Current | New | Weight increase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8" × 8" base | 1.2 mm ≈ **390 g** | 1.5 mm ≈ **488 g** | **+98 g** |
| Lower 1" tube, 3" | 1.2 mm ≈ **71 g** | 1.5 mm ≈ **88 g** | **+17 g** |
| Upper 1" tube, 3" | 1.2 mm ≈ **71 g** | 1.5 mm ≈ **88 g** | **+17 g** |
| 1.25" × 1.25" tube, 10" | 1.2 mm ≈ **298 g** | 1.2 mm ≈ **298 g** | — |
| **Total** | **\~830 g** | **\~970 g** | **+140 g** |

So you're adding only about **140 grams** to the whole structure.

 ***### Strength/stiffness***

 For the **1" × 1" tubes**, going from 1.2 → 1.5 mm is more significant than the previous 1.0 → 1.2 mm change.

 Approximate relative values:

 | Component | 1.2 mm | 1.5 mm | Increase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1" tube bending stiffness | 100% | **\~130%** | **\~30%** |
| 1" tube bending section capacity | 100% | **\~27–30% higher** |  |
| Base plate bending stiffness | 100% | **\~196%** | **\~96%** |

The base is particularly interesting: plate bending stiffness scales approximately with **thickness³**.

 So:

 **(1.5 / 1.2)³ ≈ 1.95**

 That means your 1.5 mm base is theoretically **almost twice as stiff against bending** as the 1.2 mm base, while weighing only 25% more.

 ***### I like this configuration better***

 I'd use:

```
             HP V194
          ┌───────────┐
          │ 18.5" LCD │
          └─────┬─────┘
                │
        VESA + reinforced bracket
                │
        ┌───────┴───────┐
        │ 1" × 1"       │  ← 1.5 mm
        │ 3"             │
        └───────┬───────┘
                │
       ┌────────┴────────┐
       │ 1.25" × 1.25"  │  ← 1.2 mm
       │ 10"             │
       └────────┬────────┘
                │
        ┌───────┴───────┐
        │ 1" × 1"       │  ← 1.5 mm
        │ 3"             │
        └───────┬───────┘
          ═══════════════
          8" × 8" × 1.5 mm
              BASE
```

 **This is a good balance.**

 The 1.5 mm tubes at the top and bottom make the connection points substantially more rigid, while the 1.2 mm 1.25" sleeve keeps the weight down.

 ***### One thing I'd still change***

 I'd put **two small triangular gussets at the bottom 1" tube/base connection**.

 Your 8" × 8" base is only 1.5 mm thick. The plate is much better than 1.2 mm, but the **welded tube-to-plate junction** can still flex when someone pushes the monitor.

 Something like:

```
             │
             │
             │
             │\
             │ \
             │  \  ← 30–40 mm gusset
═════════════╧═══\════════
             base
```

 Two gussets on opposite sides should make the stand feel considerably more solid.

 **For a normal HP V194 display, I would choose your new 1.5/1.2/1.5 configuration over making everything 1.5 mm.** The extra \~140 g isn't much, and you're putting the thicker material where it helps most.

 The remaining potential source of noticeable wobble is actually the **1.25" outer tube sliding around the 1" inner tube**. I'd solve that with **nylon shims + two locking bolts** rather than increasing the 10" tube thickness.

Assuming the dimensions are **nominal outside dimensions**:

 - Inner tube: **1" × 1" × 1.5 mm**
- Outer tube: **1.25" × 1.25" × 1.2 mm**

 ***### Theoretical gap***

 **1" tube outer dimension:**\
 25.4 mm

 **1.25" tube inner dimension:**\
 31.75 − (2 × 1.2) = **29.35 mm**

 Therefore:

 **29.35 − 25.4 = 3.95 mm total clearance**

 So you'd have approximately:

 - **1.98 mm clearance on each side**
- **3.95 mm total side-to-side clearance**

```
1.25" outer tube
┌──────────────────────┐
│   ← 1.98 mm →        │
│    ┌────────────┐     │
│    │ 1" × 1"    │     │
│    │  1.5 mm    │     │
│    └────────────┘     │
│        ← 1.98 mm →   │
└──────────────────────┘
```
